[warmly] Our exit sends twenty million bits each second. [short pause] There are eight million bits ahead of green. Divide eight million by twenty million: zero point four seconds. That is four hundred milliseconds, because a second contains one thousand milliseconds. Watch eight blocks leave; each block represents one million bits, not one normal packet. Every block adds fifty milliseconds. By the eighth, four hundred milliseconds have passed before green can start its own turn. A short message can inherit a surprisingly long delay.
