[warmly] Our public key contains fifty-five, but not its two prime factors. If someone discovers five and eleven, they can repeat our private-key construction: calculate forty, then find the matching power twenty-seven. For fifty-five that is easy, which is why this example protects nothing. Real RSA uses far larger carefully generated primes. Multiplying them is practical, while recovering their factors from the product is intended to be difficult. The security depends on such mathematical assumptions and on correct implementation.
