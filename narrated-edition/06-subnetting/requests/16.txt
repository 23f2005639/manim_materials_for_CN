[warmly] We started with computers needing addresses. Those addresses became rows of bits, and bits gave us simple choices. Keep a beginning fixed, then count the endings that can change. Using two more bits for group labels made four subnets, each with sixty-four addresses. The starts, the mask, and the device counts all came from that one division. Subnetting looks like a page of mysterious numbers until you see the picture underneath: one collection, a movable boundary, and smaller groups.
